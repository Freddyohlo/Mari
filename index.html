Part 1 — How did the Greeks dress?

1. Materials and colours

Greek clothes were made at home. They used wool from sheep and linen from a plant called flax. They dyed the fabric with plants and shells to get colours like white, yellow, red and blue. Purple was the most expensive colour.

La ropa griega se hacía en casa. Usaban lana de oveja y lino de una planta llamada lino. Teñían la tela con plantas y conchas para obtener colores como blanco, amarillo, rojo y azul. El morado era el color más caro.

2. The chiton

The chiton was the most common Greek clothes. It was like a long tunic made of two pieces of cloth. Women wore a long chiton; men wore a shorter one.

El chiton era la prenda griega más común. Era como una túnica larga hecha de dos piezas de tela. Las mujeres usaban un chiton largo; los hombres, uno más corto.

3. The peplos

Women also wore the peplos: a big piece of wool folded over the shoulders and held with two brooches. In winter, people wore a cloak: the himation for women and the chlamys for men.

Las mujeres también usaban el peplos: una gran pieza de lana doblada sobre los hombros y sujeta con dos broches. En invierno usaban un manto: el himation para las mujeres y la clámide para los hombres.

4. Getting dressed

They did not use buttons or zips. They used brooches called fibulae, and a belt to tie the tunic. They wore leather sandals, and women had long hair with ribbons and gold jewellery.

No usaban botones ni cierres. Usaban broches llamados fíbulas y un cinturón para sujetar la túnica. Usaban sandalias de cuero, y las mujeres llevaban el pelo largo con cintas y joyas de oro.

Part 2 — How did the Romans dress?

1. The tunica

The basic Roman clothes was the tunica, like a big T-shirt. Everybody wore it: men, women, children and slaves. Poor people wore a short tunica; rich people wore a long one.

La prenda básica romana era la túnica, como una camiseta grande. Todos la usaban: hombres, mujeres, niños y esclavos. La gente pobre usaba una túnica corta; la gente rica, una larga.

2. The toga

Only Roman citizens could wear the toga. It was a big piece of wool, about 6 metres long, worn over the tunica. It was very heavy and difficult to put on. The colour had a meaning: the toga virilis was for adult men, the toga praetexta (with a purple stripe) was for boys and important people, and the toga pulla (dark) was for funerals.

Solo los ciudadanos romanos podían usar la toga. Era una gran pieza de lana, de unos 6 metros, que se ponía sobre la túnica. Era muy pesada y difícil de poner. El color tenía un significado: la toga virilis era para hombres adultos, la toga praetexta (con franja morada) era para niños y personas importantes, y la toga pulla (oscura) era para funerales.

3. Roman women

Roman women wore a long tunica and, over it, the stola. Outside the house, they wore the palla, a big shawl. Women's clothes showed if they were married.

Las mujeres romanas usaban una túnica larga y, encima, la stola. Fuera de casa usaban la palla, un gran chal. La ropa de las mujeres mostraba si estaban casadas.

4. Shoes and accessories

Romans wore calcei (leather shoes) outside and soleae (sandals) at home. They loved jewellery: rings, bracelets and brooches.

Los romanos usaban calcei (zapatos de cuero) en la calle y soleae (sandalias) en casa. Les encantaban las joyas: anillos, pulseras y broches.

2. What clothes said about you

In Greece and Rome, clothes told people who you were: rich or poor, citizen or slave, adult or child. Clothes were like a uniform of your life.

En Grecia y Roma, la ropa decía quién eras: rico o pobre, ciudadano o esclavo, adulto o niño. La ropa era como un uniforme de tu vida.

3. Fun facts

  1. Purple dye came from sea snails and was so expensive that only emperors used it.El tinte morado venía de caracoles de mar y era tan caro que solo los emperadores lo usaban.
